## Northern Pike (Esox lucius)
- Typical 40-55 cm; record up to 150 cm / 28.4 kg; IGFA 25 kg
- Long streamlined ambush predator, pointed (duckbill) snout, large tail fin; dorsal and anal fins set far back
- Olive green back, yellow/white belly; light BAR-LIKE spots on flanks; dark spots on fins, fins sometimes reddish; lower gill cover scaleless
- Sharp backward-pointing teeth. Fight: explosive acceleration from ambush, aerial acrobatics

## Walleye (Sander vitreus) -> game name "Amber Walleye"
- Typical 30-50 cm (50 cm ~1.5 kg); max 107 cm / 13 kg; IGFA 11.34 kg (Tennessee, 1960)
- Olive back, GOLD flanks, white belly; five darker saddles across upper sides
- Large pearlescent opaque-looking eyes (reflective tapetum); large mouth with sharp teeth
- Spiny first dorsal and anal fins, spiny gill cover; WHITE lower tip of the tail (separates it from sauger)

## Channel Catfish (Ictalurus punctatus)
- Average 30+ cm, 0.9-1.8 kg; record 26 kg (58 lb, South Carolina 1964)
- Four pairs of barbels (taste buds, cannot sting); sharp serrated spines on pectoral and dorsal fins
- Source does NOT give body shape/colour; from general knowledge, to verify (FishBase): slender, silvery-blue-gray with scattered dark spots, deeply forked tail
- Fight: not described in source

## Brook Trout (Salvelinus fontinalis)
- Typical 25-65 cm, 0.3-3 kg; max 86 cm / 6.6 kg; IGFA 6.57 kg (Nipigon River, Ontario)
- Dark green to brown, olive-green back; reddish belly/lower fins (red-orange belly on spawning males)
- Light marbled VERMICULATIONS across back and flanks, reaching the tail
- RED spots each with a BLUE halo (yellow spots too); fins orange with white and black leading-edge striping
- Fight: not described (popular fly-fishing species)

## Burbot (Lota lota)
- Avg ~40 cm at maturity, max 30-120 cm, 1-12 kg; IGFA 11.4 kg (Lake Diefenbaker, 2010)
- Long, serpent-like, flattened, flat head, wide mouth; "cross between a catfish and an eel"
- Nearly scaleless; ONE chin barbel; small tube-like nostrils
- Two soft dorsal fins (first low and short, second very long), long low anal fin, ROUNDED tail, small fan-shaped pectorals
- Colour not given in source (mottled per Latin synonyms; verify). Fight: tenacious, low endurance (small fins)

## Lake Whitefish (Coregonus clupeaformis)
- Average ~1.8 kg, ~51 cm, max ~79 cm; rod-and-reel record 6.5 kg (14 lb 6 oz, Meaford, Ontario, 1984)
- Deep herring-like body, small head, "humpback" nickname
- Silver-white sides, olive/pale-green/brown back; white ventral fins; DARK rear edge on the tail
- Snout overhangs short lower jaw (mouth opens slightly downward); small adipose fin
